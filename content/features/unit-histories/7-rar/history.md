### 6 August 1967: Bunker, Operation Ballarat

*Significant* · 7 RAR in contact

Australian: 6 killed, 19 wounded. Enemy: 15 killed, 25 wounded.

Operation Ballarat, in the Hat Dich area. A Company, 7 RAR, had been inserted quietly to ambush trails to enemy camps, and its 2 Platoon killed two enemy who walked into a hasty ambush. Sent along the track after the rest of the enemy squad, the platoon ran into heavy automatic fire from a defended position held by a company of the 274th Regiment's 3rd Battalion, covering the withdrawal of its battalion from a camp nearby. The whole company was committed, supported by air strikes and by 106 Field Battery and a US battery, until the enemy withdrew after about four hours. Six Australians were killed and about twenty wounded; an estimated 15 enemy were killed and 25 wounded by the company, and many more by the artillery and air strikes.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=586) · Report's sources: Intsum, AWM95 1/4/55.pdf p172; AWM279 569/R4/66; On the Offensive pg210-216; Horner Arty pg 483;

### 10 November 1967: Security, Operation Santa Fe

*Typical: security* · 7 RAR in contact

Australian: 2 killed, 6 wounded.

A command-detonated Claymore mine was fired into 2 Platoon, A Company, 7 RAR's base from outside the perimeter, probably aimed at the sound of its radio, killing two Australians and wounding six. Sweeping the area, the platoon killed two Viet Cong and captured two rifles. The company commander noted that a helicopter visit an hour earlier may have given the position away, and that the men had run to the wounded without waiting for a sweep, risking a second mine.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=721) · Report's sources: Intsum, 1/4/69.pdf p156; AWM109 VN Records Ops 1ATF; On the Offensive pg261; AWM95 7-7-15.pdf 7 RAR Nov 67 pg55; AWM95 7-7-20.pdf 7 RAR Jan 68 pg8, 29-30 Contact Rept

### 27 November 1967: Bunker, Operation Forrest

*Significant* · 7 RAR in contact

Australian: 2 killed, 22 wounded. Enemy: 4 killed, 4 wounded.

Acting on SAS intelligence, D Company, 7 RAR, assaulted an occupied Viet Cong base camp while B Company waited in ambush on the escape routes. 10 and 12 Platoons took several bunkers, but when they reached the centre of the camp the enemy, using communication trenches, came round behind them and attacked from three sides, firing rocket-propelled grenades into the tree canopy to rain shrapnel on the Australians. Close quarters ruled out artillery and helicopter gunships, and grenade launchers and a machine-gun failed. The fighting, in heavy rain, died away at nightfall; two Australians were shot dead and about 21 wounded by shrapnel, several of whom stayed on duty. The camp had been used by the D445 Battalion and the Chau Duc district company.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=737) · Report's sources: Intsum, 1/4/69.pdf p184; AWM279 569/R4/77; On the Offensive pg263; AWM95 7-7-15.pdf 7 RAR Nov 67 pg102-106; AWM95 7-2-46.pdf 2 RAR pg102; AWM95 7-7-23.pdf pg114 Contact Rept;

### 27 January 1968: Bunker, Operation Coburg

*Significant* · 7 RAR in contact

Australian: 1 killed, 10 wounded. Enemy: 5 killed, 5 wounded.

10 Platoon, D Company, 7 RAR, unknowingly approached an enemy camp held by more than a company, and the forward section was hit as it crossed a deep gully. With the platoon nearly out of ammunition and under grenade-launcher and mortar fire, helicopter gunships struck the enemy only 30 metres away, and the rest of the company came up, assaulted both flanks and cleared the camp as the enemy withdrew in disorder. One Australian was killed and ten wounded, mostly by grenades and rockets. The enemy, of the C238 Battalion, had been massing for an attack on Long Binh.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=860) · Report's sources: Intsum, AWM95 1/4/80.pdf p159; AWM95 7-7-19.pdf pg100, 102; AWM95 7-7-23.pdf pg123, 186 Contact Rept;

### 29 January 1968: Ambush, Operation Coburg

*Significant* · 7 RAR in contact

Australian: 1 killed, 7 wounded. Enemy: 8 killed.

2 Platoon, A Company, 7 RAR, was holding an ambush when a large body of enemy approached. Claymores and machine-guns killed four of the first five into the killing ground, but 20 or more enemy fought back hard with rifles, machine-guns and rocket-propelled grenades. One Australian, the platoon's medic, died of his wounds and seven were wounded, many by rocket shrapnel; eight enemy were killed.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=875) · Report's sources: Intsum, AWM95 1/4/80.pdf p163; On the Offensive pg296; AWM95 7-7-19.pdf 7 RAR Jan 68 pg114; AWM95 7-7-23.pdf pg124, 136, 139 Contact Rept;

### 7 February 1968: Bunker, Operation Coburg

*Significant* · 7 RAR in contact

Australian: 14 wounded. Enemy: 3 killed, 5 wounded.

After air strikes and gunships had hit an enemy camp, C Company, 7 RAR, went in by a wide flanking move. The camp seemed deserted until Viet Cong in four large, well-hidden bunkers opened fire at ten metres, pinning the leading sections in crossfire; anti-tank rockets had little effect on the bunkers. The company pulled back 15 metres and brought artillery, mortars and a gunship strike in very close, after which the enemy withdrew in good order. Fourteen Australians were wounded, none seriously, six staying on duty overnight; three enemy were killed and five wounded, and the company commander thought more.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=966) · Report's sources: AWM290, Item 18 part 3, 7RAR Operation Coburg, Feb 68; AWM95 7-7-21.pdf 7 RAR Feb 68 pg38, 41, 45; AWM95 7-7-23.pdf pg183 Contact Rept;

### 22 April 1970: Bunker, Operation Concrete

*Significant* · 7 RAR in contact

Australian: 1 killed, 5 wounded. Enemy: 4 killed.

B Company, 7 RAR, fought a bunker system of 18 bunkers along the bank of the Song Rai. To let 5 Platoon break off and evacuate its wounded, a patrol from company headquarters went in to draw fire and took casualties, and then a section of 4 Platoon moved up to assault a line of bunkers so headquarters could get its own wounded out, all under intense rocket and rifle fire, with helicopter gunships firing 10 to 15 metres ahead of the troops. Everyone was brought out and an air strike hit the bunkers; tanks could not get through the jungle before dark, and by morning the enemy had gone. One Australian died of wounds and five were wounded; four enemy bodies were found later.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=3770) · Report's sources: AWM 290, 23 Op Concrete 1; WM109 Ops 1ATF \[C\]; AWM95 7-7-37.pdf 7 RAR pg9; AWM95 7-7-38.pdf 7 RAR pg36-7; AWM95 7-7-39.pdf 7 RAR pg45; AWM95 7-7-44.pdf 7 RAR pg65 Contact Rept;

### 21 May 1970: Bunker, Operation Concrete

*Significant* · 7 RAR in contact

Australian: 1 killed, 12 wounded. Enemy: 5 killed, 3 wounded.

A spotter aircraft directed 1 Platoon, A Company, 7 RAR, and the reconnaissance platoon to an occupied bunker system 800 metres away, and they went in by armoured personnel carrier behind tanks of 1 Armoured Regiment. The infantry attacked both flanks and the tanks the centre, and took some bunkers, but met intense rocket and rifle fire; a rocket hit a machine-gun team, killing the gunner, and 1 Platoon pulled back 75 metres with its wounded. The reconnaissance platoon did not get the order to withdraw and fought on, and a carrier was hit and towed out. One Australian was killed and twelve wounded; five Viet Cong were killed and three captured.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=3869) · Report's sources: AWM 290 23 Concrete; AWM103, R723/1/1 Part 2A, Reports - general - Contact or incident after action report; Scorpion Stings pg225; AWM95 7-7-44.pdf 7 RAR pg55 Contact Rept; AWM121, item 16, ARMD Incidents SVN no86; AWM95 7-7-42.pdf 7 RAR pg8

### 6 June 1970: Patrol, Operation Concrete

*Typical: patrol* · 7 RAR in contact

Australian: 3 killed, 1 wounded.

9 Platoon, C Company, 7 RAR, fired on an enemy soldier in the sandhills south-west of Phuoc Hai, who fled leaving a heavy blood trail. Following the trail at first light, the patrol set off an M16 mine that the enemy had laid where a follow-up was likely to go. Two Australians were killed, one died of wounds and one was wounded.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=3925) · Report's sources: Intsum, AWM95 1/4/192.pdf p105; AWM109 Ops 1ATF \[C\]; AWM95 7-7-45.pdf 7 RAR pg36;37

### 31 December 1970: Ambush, Operation Cung Chung 3

*Significant* · 7 RAR in contact

Enemy: 21 killed, 9 wounded.

An ambush by B Company, 7 RAR, with a section of carriers of 1 Troop, B Squadron, 3 Cavalry Regiment, held its fire until about 45 Viet Cong filled the killing ground before dawn, then fired all its Claymores and every weapon it had. The enemy fought back with rifles, machine-guns and rockets and tried again and again to recover their dead. There were no Australian casualties; 21 Viet Cong were killed and nine wounded, and a large haul of weapons, a radio and important documents was captured. The dead included the second in command of the D445 Battalion, its political officer and several of its junior leaders.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=4265) · Report's sources: Bravo Seven, Aust Inf Volxvii No3 Sept 71; Scorpion Stings pg247; Intsum, AWM95 1/4/206.pdf p270; AWM95 7-7-65.pdf 7 RAR Dec 70 pg16; AWM95 7-7-72.pdf 7 RAR Dec 70 pg112 Contact Rept

### 16 January 1971: Ambush, Operation Cung Chung 3

*Typical: ambush* · 7 RAR in contact

Australian: 1 killed, 1 wounded. Enemy: 5 killed, 7 wounded.

6 Platoon, B Company, 7 RAR, heard about 15 enemy pass 20 metres away but let them go because the ambush was badly aligned, and re-sited it along the track. When about 20 more came the other way, it let seven into the killing zone and opened fire at five metres. A dying Viet Cong threw a grenade into the machine-gun pit, killing one Australian and wounding one. Five enemy, a food-carrying party of the Long Dat district force, were killed, with more blood trails, and packs and food were captured.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=4284) · Report's sources: Intsum, AWM95 1/4/209.pdf p58; AWM95 7-7-68.pdf 7 RAR Jan 71 pg15; AWM95 7-7-70.pdf 7 RAR Jan 71 pg104; AWM95 7-7-72.pdf 7 RAR Jan 71 pg118 Contact Rept

### 19 February 1971: Ambush, Operation Phoi Hop

*Last* · 7 RAR in contact

Enemy: 2 killed.

Fresh enemy tracks in the Binh Chau area led B Company, 7 RAR, to set an ambush. The sentry saw two armed enemy and shot both; one died at once, and the other got 200 metres before dying and was found in the follow-up. Two rifles were captured.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=4335) · Report's sources: AWM109 Ops 1ATF \[P\]; Intsum; AWM95 7-7-71.pdf 7 RAR Feb 71 pg12; Contact Rept pg40; Intsum, AWM95 1/4/212.pdf p220
