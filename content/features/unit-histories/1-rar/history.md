## Notable contacts

### 29 May 1965: Deployment

*First* · 1 RAR in contact

1 RAR's advance guard arrived at Bien Hoa, and the rest of the battalion followed by air and aboard HMAS Sydney over the next weeks. Its soldiers first went out on patrols and ambushes with the US 2/503rd Battalion, and by mid-June were patrolling on their own, with nothing to report; a troop of armoured personnel carriers of the Prince of Wales Light Horse joined it. Its first shots were fired by the mortar platoon on 25 June, and its first casualties came the next day, when a grenade went off by accident, killing three and wounding seven, with one American killed. The first wounded by enemy action was a Light Horse officer, hit by harassing fire on 28 June.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=10016) · Report's sources: AWM95, item 7/1/26, 1 Battalion, Royal Australian Regiment, Narrative and Annexes 30 Apr-30 Jun 1965, pp. 5-15.

### 26 November 1965: Assault, Operation New Life

*Significant* · 1 RAR in contact

Enemy: 4 killed.

C Company, 1 RAR, carried in the armoured personnel carriers of 1 APC Troop, Prince of Wales Light Horse, made a frontal assault along the road into the village of Vo Xu, with A Company in support and B and D Companies giving diversionary fire. It met light fire on entering the village and quickly secured its objective, the south-west of Vo Xu; two Viet Cong were thought to have escaped through tunnels.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=10441) · Report's sources: AWM95, item 7/1/39, 1 Battalion Royal Australian Regiment, Narrative, Annexes, 1-30 Nov 1965, p. 22-23.

### 29 November 1965: Patrol, Operation New Life

*Significant* · 1 RAR in contact

Australian: 1 killed, 1 wounded. Enemy: 4 killed.

1 RAR moved into Vo Xu village with little trouble; 52 Viet Cong were captured and seven more gave themselves up. As B Company's cordon neared the south-east corner, 4 Platoon was fired on with machine-guns and rifles from across the La Nga River by an enemy group thought to be covering others escaping by boat. The platoon returned fire, and mortars, artillery and helicopters were used; about four enemy were thought killed, but no bodies were recovered. One Australian corporal was wounded and later died, and another soldier was wounded.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=10442) · Report's sources: AWM95, item 7/1/39, 1 Battalion Royal Australian Regiment, Narrative, Annexes, 1-30 Nov 1965, p. 25. 2LT Bolitho's After Action Report is at AWM95, item 7/1/46, 1 Battalion Royal Australian Regiment, Annex Material, 1-30 Nov 1965, p. 38.

### 10 January 1966: Installation, Operation 1/66

*Typical: installation* · 1 RAR in contact

Australian: 1 killed.

Engineers of 3 Field Troop pumped smoke and CS gas into tunnels found by B Company, 1 RAR, to reveal other entrances and deny them to the enemy. An engineer corporal inspecting a shaft was overcome by the gas and lack of air, and was dead when rescuers reached him; two of those who tried to reach him were evacuated. Documents, food, medical stores and a booby-trapped trapdoor were found in the tunnels and caches.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=10079) · Report's sources: Major Ian McFarlane's Operational Analysis is at AWM95, item 7/1/56, 1 Battalion, Royal Australian Regiment, Annexes, 1-31 Jan 1966, p. 12-19.

### 10 January 1966: Patrol, Operation 1/66

*Typical: patrol* · 1 RAR in contact

Australian: 2 killed, 6 wounded.

12 Platoon, D Company, 1 RAR, clearing the ground for a battalion harbour, walked into open country covered by a Viet Cong ambush of four to seven men in well-built bunkers, who opened with grenades and automatic fire. Several men were wounded, including the platoon commander, and two medics were killed going forward to help them; a section sweeping to the east was pinned down, and a flanking attack by the company's support section met no opposition. Two Australians were killed and six wounded over an hour and twenty minutes. The lessons drawn were to avoid open ground and keep medics back until it was safe.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=10070) · Report's sources: Sgt Carnes' After Action Report is at AWM95, item 7/1/56, 1 Battalion, Royal Australian Regiment, Annexes, 1-31 Jan 1966, p. 44-45.

### 13 May 1968: Security, Operation Toan Thang 1

*Significant* · 1 RAR in contact

Australian: 9 killed, 28 wounded. Enemy: 55 killed.

Fire Support Base Coral, north-east of Saigon, was attacked by an estimated battalion with very heavy and accurate mortar fire, rockets and a ground assault. The enemy overran a 105 mm gun position and 1 RAR's mortar platoon, and were only driven out by dawn, with the support of a gunship aircraft and helicopter gunships; 1 RAR's rifle companies were out in ambush positions one or two kilometres away. Nine Australians were killed and 28 wounded; 55 North Vietnamese were counted dead and one captured.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=1233) · Report's sources: Intsum, AWM95 1/4/100.pdf p93; Lex McAulay; On the Offensive pg358

### 14 May 1968: Ambush, Operation Toan Thang 1

*Typical: ambush* · 1 RAR in contact

Australian: 1 killed, 1 wounded. Enemy: 7 killed.

A Viet Cong group broke contact with 2 Platoon, A Company, 1 RAR, when a gunship arrived. Driven by armoured personnel carriers, those who withdrew north ran into 7 Platoon, C Company's ambush and fought back. One Australian was killed and two wounded; seven Viet Cong were killed, one wounded man captured, and weapons taken.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=1255) · Report's sources: Intsum, AWM95 1/4/100.pdf p95; AWM 569/R4/93; McAulay pg130; AWM95 7-1-78.pdf pt 1 1 RAR May 68 pg15, 126

### 16 May 1968: Security, Operation Toan Thang 1

*Significant* · 1 RAR in contact

Australian: 7 killed, 19 wounded. Enemy: 38 killed, 10 wounded.

Fire Support Base Coral was attacked a second time by an estimated regiment: heavy mortar and rocket fire, then a ground assault. It was beaten off with the help of a gunship aircraft, helicopter gunships and an air strike on an enemy anti-aircraft gun. Seven Australians were killed and about 19 wounded, and two Americans wounded; 37 North Vietnamese were counted dead and one captured. Later intelligence assessed that only 100 of the 790 attackers came through unhurt.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=1272) · Report's sources: Intsum, AWM95 1/4/100.pdf p99; McAulay pg176; Scorpion Stings pg110-112

### 22 May 1968: Security, Operation Toan Thang 1

*Typical: security* · 1 RAR in contact

About 30 mortar rounds were fired at Fire Support Base Coral but landed outside the perimeter. There was no damage and there were no casualties.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=1306) · Report's sources: Intsum, AWM95 1/4/100.pdf p111; AWM95 7-1-78.pdf pt 1, 1 RAR May 68 pg21

### 26 May 1968: Security, Operation Toan Thang 1

*Significant* · 1 RAR in contact

Australian: 1 killed.

To draw attention from the attack on Balmoral, the enemy fired on Fire Support Base Coral for 30 minutes with about 60 mortar bombs and 20 recoilless-rifle rounds. One Australian was killed and two wounded; enemy casualties were not known.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=1324) · Report's sources: McAulay pg226; AWM95 7-1-78.pdf pt 1, 1 RAR May 68 pg22; Intsum, AWM95 1/4/100.pdf p119;

### 26 May 1968: Bunker, Operation Toan Thang 1

*Significant* · 1 RAR in contact

Enemy: 17 killed.

Tanks of C Squadron, 1 Armoured Regiment, and D Company, 1 RAR, attacked a bunker system near Fire Support Base Coral that had been found the day before. The infantry protected the tanks while they cleared the jungle with canister rounds and blew up or crushed the bunkers; the infantry also used flamethrowers, and rocket-propelled grenades had no effect on the tanks. By dusk 14 to 18 bunkers had been destroyed without Australian loss, and at least seven enemy killed, with more buried in their bunkers.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=1325) · Report's sources: McAulay pg246-253; AWM95 7-1-78.pdf pt 1, 1 RAR May 68 pg22; AWM95 7-1-79.pdf 1 RAR Jun 68 pg75; AWM95 2-3-9.pdf pg40 Contact Report;

### 30 May 1968: Bunker, Operation Toan Thang 1

*Significant* · 1 RAR in contact

Australian: 1 killed, 7 wounded. Enemy: 24 killed, 8 wounded.

On a reconnaissance in force near Coral, C Company, 1 RAR, was pinned down by heavy mortar, machine-gun and rocket fire from a bunker system, at ranges of 10 to 15 metres, too close for gunships and artillery to help much, and carriers could not get through the jungle. Two tanks arrived from Coral and an assault destroyed eight bunkers; a carrier was hit by a rocket while taking out the wounded. The company then broke contact for air strikes. One Australian was killed and seven wounded; 24 enemy were killed and more probably killed, and five bodies were found when the company returned days later.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=1333) · Report's sources: Scorpion Stings pg119; McAulay pg289; On the Offensive pg394; AWM95 7-1-78 pt 1 .pdf 1 RAR May 68 pg25; AWM95 7-1-79.pdf 1 RAR Jun 68 pg71; AWM95 2-3-9.pdf pg53 Contact Rept AFV;

### 22 August 1968: Attack, Operation Nowra

*Significant* · 1 RAR in contact

Australian: 12 wounded. Enemy: 40 killed, 8 wounded.

About 100 enemy, of the D445 Battalion, attacked Long Dien with recoilless rifles, rocket-propelled grenades and automatic weapons. B and C Companies of 1 RAR, with tanks of C Squadron, 1 Armoured Regiment, carriers and helicopter gunships, fought a company-sized force in the town and another group on Route 23, in contacts that went on all day. About eleven Australians were wounded, most of C Company's by a 60 mm mortar; 29 to 40 enemy were recorded killed.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=1503) · Report's sources: Intsum, AWM1/4/113 pt3.pdf p44; See Urban Warfare Rept. AWM95 7-1-86.pdf 1 RAR Aug 68 pg15-16; AWM95 2-3-12.pdf pg43 Contact Rept AFV;

*Last* · 1 RAR in contact

D Company made contact with three enemy moving south, without result.
