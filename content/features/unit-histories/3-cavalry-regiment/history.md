### 12 June 1966: Patrol, Operation Hardihood

*First* · Armoured personnel carriers for 5 RAR

Australian: 1 killed, 3 wounded.

D Company, 5 RAR, was sweeping the north-east of Hoa Long for enemy mortar positions when its force came under light mortar fire. One Australian was killed and three wounded, from D and E Platoon, 1 APC Squadron and 1 Field Squadron. Radar placed the mortars at Long Phuoc village, and artillery fired on them with unknown results.

### 18 August 1966: Patrol, Operation Smithfield (original - Vendetta)

*Significant* · Armoured personnel carriers for 6 RAR

Australian: 17 killed, 19 wounded. Enemy: 245 killed, 350 wounded.

The Battle of Long Tan. D Company, 6 RAR, 108 strong, was following fresh tracks east of Nui Dat when 11 Platoon met a few Viet Cong and then a much larger force. From about 5.30 pm the company was attacked by at least a company, later found to be far more, under heavy machine-gun and mortar fire in heavy rain, and held on until A Company arrived in armoured personnel carriers after 7 pm. Close artillery fire broke up many of the attacks; low cloud ruled out air strikes, and helicopters flew in ammunition and took out the wounded. Seventeen Australians were killed and nineteen wounded; 245 enemy were recorded killed. The company commander afterwards reported that the M60 machine-guns jammed in the mud and that the men needed to carry more ammunition.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=220) · Report's sources: To Long Tan; Sitrep, AWM95 1/4/6.pdf p56-8; AWM 102, 42 6RAR After Action Report, op. Smithfield, 18 -21 August 1966, Nui Dat (Battle of Long Tan)

### 17 February 1967: Bunker, Operation Bribie

*Significant* · Armoured personnel carriers for 6 RAR

Australian: 8 killed, 21 wounded. Enemy: 50 killed, 13 wounded.

Operation Bribie. After A Company, 6 RAR, found a Viet Cong company and its heavy weapons company dug into bunkers, B Company was ordered to assault the position. It was pinned down by fire from all sides, including heavy machine-guns, Claymore mines and mortars; four air strikes were called in, and carriers of 2 Troop, A Squadron, 3 Cavalry Regiment, went in to bring out the wounded, one being disabled by recoilless-rifle fire. After four hours both companies withdrew into the battalion's night position, and the enemy left during the night. Eight Australians were killed and 21 wounded; about 50 enemy were recorded killed. The battalion's commander said that tanks could have taken the position with little difficulty.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=447) · Report's sources: AWM 279, 723/R5/34; Scorpion Stings pg48-54; On the Offensive pg114. AWM95 7-6-11 pdf 6 RAR Op Bribie; AWM95 7-6-14.pdf pg189 Contact Rept;

### 20 July 1967: Security

*Typical: security* · 3 Cav Regt in contact

Australian: 4 wounded.

3 Troop's position at Duc Thanh was shelled by 60 mm mortars. Artillery at Nui Dat returned fire and silenced them. No carriers were damaged, and four men were slightly wounded.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=568) · Report's sources: When the Scorpion Stings pg64; AWM95 2-4-7.pdf pg8

### 1 February 1968: Security, Operation Coburg

*Significant* · Armoured personnel carriers for 3 RAR

Australian: 1 killed, 19 wounded. Enemy: 42 killed, 5 wounded.

During the Tet Offensive a Viet Cong battalion seized much of Ba Ria, a town of 15,000, and the province headquarters asked 1 ATF for help. A Company, 3 RAR, less a platoon, with nine armoured personnel carriers of 3 Troop, A Squadron, 3 Cavalry Regiment, was rushed in by carrier to protect the US compound and other key buildings, and fought through a town full of uncleared houses, infiltrators and rocket teams. One Australian was killed and nineteen wounded; 42 enemy were recorded killed.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=903) · Report's sources: R Hall and A T Ross,

### 16 May 1968: Security, Operation Toan Thang 1

*Significant* · Armoured personnel carriers for 1 RAR

Australian: 7 killed, 19 wounded. Enemy: 38 killed, 10 wounded.

Fire Support Base Coral was attacked a second time by an estimated regiment: heavy mortar and rocket fire, then a ground assault. It was beaten off with the help of a gunship aircraft, helicopter gunships and an air strike on an enemy anti-aircraft gun. Seven Australians were killed and about 19 wounded, and two Americans wounded; 37 North Vietnamese were counted dead and one captured. Later intelligence assessed that only 100 of the 790 attackers came through unhurt.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=1272) · Report's sources: Intsum, AWM95 1/4/100.pdf p99; McAulay pg176; Scorpion Stings pg110-112

### 26 May 1968: Security, Operation Toan Thang 1

*Significant* · Armoured personnel carriers for 3 RAR

Australian: 3 killed, 16 wounded. Enemy: 6 killed, 30 wounded.

The 165th North Vietnamese Regiment attacked Fire Support Base Balmoral, held by 3 RAR with tanks and carriers: heavy mortar fire, followed by a ground attack by about a company, supported by rockets and machine-guns, from an estimated battalion group. Three Australians were killed and sixteen wounded; six enemy were recorded killed.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=1323) · Report's sources: McAulay pg237-8; Scorpion Stings pg 114-116; On the Offensive pg 384; AWM95 2-3-9.pdf pg36 Contact Rept AFV;

### 28 May 1968: Security, Operation Toan Thang 1

*Significant* · Armoured personnel carriers for 3 RAR

Australian: 1 killed, 8 wounded. Enemy: 55 killed, 10 wounded.

The North Vietnamese renewed their attack on Fire Support Base Balmoral with a heavy mortar and rocket bombardment and a ground attack by two battalions of the 165th Regiment. The attack broke up on the wire under artillery and helicopter gunship fire. One Australian was killed and six to eight wounded; about 47 to 55 enemy were killed and six captured.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=1330) · Report's sources: AWM95 2-3-9.pdf pg46 Contact Rept AFV; Scorpion Stings pg118 ; Intsum, AWM95 1/4/100.pdf p122;

### 22 August 1968: Attack, Operation Nowra

*Significant* · Armoured personnel carriers for 1 RAR

Australian: 12 wounded. Enemy: 40 killed, 8 wounded.

About 100 enemy, of the D445 Battalion, attacked Long Dien with recoilless rifles, rocket-propelled grenades and automatic weapons. B and C Companies of 1 RAR, with tanks of C Squadron, 1 Armoured Regiment, carriers and helicopter gunships, fought a company-sized force in the town and another group on Route 23, in contacts that went on all day. About eleven Australians were wounded, most of C Company's by a 60 mm mortar; 29 to 40 enemy were recorded killed.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=1503) · Report's sources: Intsum, AWM1/4/113 pt3.pdf p44; See Urban Warfare Rept. AWM95 7-1-86.pdf 1 RAR Aug 68 pg15-16; AWM95 2-3-12.pdf pg43 Contact Rept AFV;

### 27 March 1969: Transport, Operation Federal

*Typical: transport* · 3 Cav Regt in contact

Australian: 20 wounded.

A Company, 5 RAR, with A Squadron, 3 Cavalry Regiment, was escorting a convoy of trucks up Route 15 in Bien Hoa when two US lorries hit mines 50 metres apart. About 20 Australians were wounded, half of whom stayed on duty, and two Americans.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=2162) · Report's sources: Intsum, AWM95 1/4/144.pdf p121; Scorpion Stings pg157; AWM95 7-5-20.pdf pg27, 7-5-21.pdf p128;

### 6 June 1969: Contact, Operation Hammer

*Significant* · Armoured personnel carriers for 5 RAR

Australian: 1 killed, 10 wounded. Enemy: 43 killed, 6 wounded.

The Battle of Binh Ba. B and D Companies of 5 RAR, with tanks of B Squadron, 1 Armoured Regiment, carriers of B Squadron, 3 Cavalry Regiment, and local Regional and Popular Force troops, fought an estimated 90 enemy in the village of Binh Ba from late morning until evening. One Australian was killed and eight to ten wounded, and two Centurion tanks were damaged; 27 enemy were counted dead, more were recorded later, and two were captured.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=2496) · Report's sources: Sitrep, AWM95 1/4/156.pdf p16; Analysis of Aust Urban Engagements in Vn; Scorpion Stings pg169-78;

### 5 August 1969: Ambush, Operation U/K

*Typical: ambush* · 3 Cav Regt in contact

Enemy: 13 killed, 4 wounded.

Four armoured personnel carriers of 3 Troop, B Squadron, 3 Cavalry Regiment, set an ambush near the beach, one of several in the area, and fired on 20 to 25 Viet Cong in the killing zone. Thirteen were killed, four wounded and one captured, with no Australian casualties. Weapons, documents and packs of food and supplies were captured.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=2746) · Report's sources: Scorpion Stings pg185; Sitrep, AWM95 1/4/164.pdf p17

### 12 June 1971: Patrol, Operation Overlord

*Typical: patrol* · 3 Cav Regt in contact

Australian: 8 killed, 2 wounded.

Three armoured personnel carriers of A Squadron, 3 Cavalry Regiment, carrying men of D and E Platoon, were patrolling a track when the leading driver stopped at an illumination canister he suspected was part of a mine. The enemy, of the 274th Regiment, opened fire, and a rocket-propelled grenade hit a pile of Claymore mines on the back of the last carrier, which exploded, killing its crew and five of D and E Platoon. Eight Australians were killed in all, and two wounded.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=4539) · Report's sources: Intsum AWM95 1-4-225.pdf pg122; AWM109 Ops 1ATF \[O\]; Scorpion Stings pg265;

### 12 October 1971: Security, Operation South Ward

*Last* · 3 Cav Regt in contact

Armoured personnel carriers of A Squadron, 3 Cavalry Regiment, fired on two enemy after a light was seen at the perimeter. The enemy fired one shot, and a sweep found nothing.
