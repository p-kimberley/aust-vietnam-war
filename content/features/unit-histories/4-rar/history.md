## Notable contacts

Each report is summarised in plain English by an AI model from the original, which is full of abbreviations and soldiers' shorthand; the original is on the Battle Map.

### 4 June 1968: Ambush, Operation Toan Thang 1

*First* · 4 RAR in contact

Enemy: 1 killed.

4 Platoon, B Company, set an ambush 50 metres from a track behind a paddy bund, as close as it could get under cover. The machine-gunner let a party of six enemy right into the ambush before opening fire, setting off four Claymores; one enemy was killed and the rest dropped into a creek and escaped. The platoon had arrived late and had no time to site its Claymores well, and the company commander noted that any patrol leaving in daylight was liable to be reported to the Viet Cong by locals.

### 13 September 1968: Bunker, Operation Hawkesbury

*Significant* · 4 RAR in contact

Australian: 3 killed, 6 wounded. Enemy: 2 killed.

7 Platoon, C Company, 4 RAR, found what looked like an abandoned bunker system of about 20 interlocking bunkers in thick jungle, and quickly organised a two-section assault. The first bunkers were cleared, but halfway in both sections came under very disciplined rifle fire, and the casualties came in clearing three main bunkers. Two Australians were killed, one died of wounds and six were wounded; two enemy were killed and rice, packs, medical kits and documents captured. The platoon concluded it should have reconnoitred the bunkers more carefully before attacking.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=1534) · Report's sources: Intsum, AWM1/4/116.pdf p161; Contact Report AWM 723/1/72; AWM95 7-4-31.pdf pg27; AWM95 7-4-32.pdf pg206 Contact Rept

### 8 October 1968: Bunker, Operation TRACK Duster

*Significant* · 4 RAR in contact

Australian: 2 killed, 6 wounded.

12 Platoon, D Company, 4 RAR, was working through an area full of enemy camps. Its commander, leading a small group along a side track, was seen by enemy in a hidden camp 35 metres away, who set off two mines, one a mortar shell and the other a Claymore, threw a grenade and opened fire. Two Australians were killed and six wounded, all by the mines and grenade; the platoon attacked and swept the camp, but the enemy had gone.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=1616) · Report's sources: Contact Report AWM 723/1/72; Intel V-dat base; Scorpion Stings pg 138; AWM95 7-4-32.pdf pg22; AWM95 7-4-34.pdf pg118 Contact Rept; Intsum, AWM95 1/4/121.pdf p88

### 23 December 1968: Patrol, Operation Goodwood

*Typical: patrol* · 4 RAR in contact

Australian: 1 killed, 5 wounded.

A 28-man platoon of C Company, 4 RAR, was sent to find 100 to 200 enemy seen from the air. Half went forward to reconnoitre along a paddy field while the rest waited with three armoured personnel carriers as a reaction force; crossing a tree line, the forward group was hit by a command-detonated Claymore. The reaction force came straight down the paddy, fired on the area and brought the group out, and mortars were fired. One Australian was killed and five wounded.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=1844) · Report's sources: Intsum, AWM95 1/4/131.pdf p125; Contact Report AWM 723/1/72; AWM95 7-4-34.pdf pg11; AWM95 7-4-35.pdf pg224 Contact Rept;

### 1 February 1969: Security, Operation Goodwood

*Typical: security* · 4 RAR in contact

Enemy: 1 killed, 2 wounded.

5 Platoon, B Company, 4 RAR, fired a Claymore at several enemy moving round its perimeter, wounding one, who moaned through the night. The enemy made several attempts to rescue him, firing bursts into the perimeter, and were answered with grenade launchers and rifles. In the morning a body and a rifle were found, with two more blood trails.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=2009) · Report's sources: Intsum, AWM95 1/4/140.pdf p60; AWM95 7-4-36.pdf pg18-19

### 15 February 1969: Ambush, Operation Goodwood

*Typical: ambush* · 4 RAR in contact

Enemy: 6 killed.

Acting on an informant's information, 10 Platoon, D Company, 4 RAR, ambushed four or five boats carrying 12 to 15 enemy. Two boats were sunk and another drifted downstream; the enemy fired back from the south bank, and artillery hit their escape routes. Six enemy were killed.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=2061) · Report's sources: AWM95 7-4-36.pdf pg11, 31; Intsum, AWM95 1/4/140.pdf p85

### 16 February 1969: Bunker, Operation Goodwood

*Significant* · In 1 Armd Regt's contact

Australian: 2 killed, 8 wounded. Enemy: 6 killed.

1 Troop, B Squadron, 1 Armoured Regiment, and B Company, 4 RAR, were fired on from bunkers, and attacked them into rocket-propelled grenades, machine-guns and Claymore mines, with air strikes and gunships in support. One tank was badly damaged by four or five rockets. Two Australians were killed and eight wounded, B Company's mostly by Claymores and the tanks' by rockets; W Company joined, and both companies pulled back for heavy artillery and to be resupplied with ammunition. Six enemy were killed.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=2064) · Report's sources: Intsum, AWM95 1/4/140.pdf p87; AWM95 7-4-36.pdf pg11-12, 31-33,

### 7 March 1969: Ambush, Operation MAT

*Significant* · 4 RAR in contact

Australian: 4 killed, 2 wounded. Enemy: 1 killed.

Three Australians and 22 South Vietnamese Regional Force soldiers set an ambush in the paddy fields 150 metres from the tree line of Dat Do. Within 15 minutes the enemy opened fire from the trees with machine-guns, rifles and rockets, pinning the patrol behind the paddy bunds, and several were killed as it tried to withdraw, among them an Australian corporal; the enemy then advanced by fire and movement. Mortar fire from 4 RAR at the Horseshoe stopped them, and they withdrew leaving one dead. The platoon had ambushed from the same place too often, and the enemy had learned its habits.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=2135) · Report's sources: AWM290 4/R723/1/3 Pt1 Contact Report; AWM109 R723/1/1/2 Pt2, HQ 1 ATF Reports General, Contact Rept;

### 9 April 1969: Ambush, Operation Overlander

*Significant* · 4 RAR in contact

Enemy: 7 killed, 3 wounded.

4 RAR's Anti-Tank Platoon, in ambush at night, fired Claymores and machine-guns at enemy movement, lighting the area with hand flares, artillery and mortars. When moaning and movement were heard again, it fired grenades and machine-guns once more. The sweep at first light found seven bodies, blood trails, a drag mark and six rifles.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=2241) · Report's sources: Intsum AWM95 1/4/148.pdf p108; AWM95 7-4-38.pdf pg7,20

### 29 July 1971: Bunker, Operation Iron Fox

*Typical: bunker* · 4 RAR in contact

Australian: 3 wounded. Enemy: 3 killed.

7 Platoon, C Company, 4 RAR, was following a track from an empty bunker system when its forward section crossed a steep gully and saw a bunker; as the section commander went forward, an enemy came out and the fight began. The platoon came under rifle fire from the bunkers, Claymore mines fired from the trees and then rocket-propelled grenades, and after about an hour withdrew by fire and movement, with artillery keeping the enemy in their bunkers. Three Australians were wounded; at least three enemy were heard crying out from wounds. Many shells and grenades failed to go off in the soft ground, and two machine-guns broke down, but the rifle-fired grenades proved their worth.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=4609) · Report's sources: AWM288, R569/1/2, Operation Iron Fox; AWM109 Ops 1 ATF \[I\]; AWM95 7-4-48.pdf pg253 Contact Rept; Intsum, AWM95 1/4/229.pdf p188

### 30 July 1971: Bunker, Operation Iron Fox

*Significant* · 4 RAR in contact

Australian: 1 killed, 5 wounded. Enemy: 12 killed.

D Company, 4 RAR/NZ, with tanks of C Squadron, 1 Armoured Regiment, was searching for the 274th Regiment's 1st Battalion. Just after the tanks had left, 10 Platoon came under rocket fire from a bunker, and fire from the system grew intense; 12 Platoon killed three enemy fleeing another bunker. A troop of tanks came back and, slowed at first by thick jungle and the need to find the infantry, pushed 100 metres ahead of them through the bunkers, firing main guns and machine-guns, and cleared the system with the infantry. One Australian was killed and four or five wounded; twelve enemy were killed.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=4611) · Report's sources: Intsum, AWM95 1/4/229.pdf p192; AWM 109 Ops 1ATF \[I\]; AWM288, R569/1/2, Operation Iron Fox; AWM95 7-4-48.pdf pg257 Contact Rept;

### 21 September 1971: Bunker, Operation Ivanhoe

*Significant* · 4 RAR in contact

Australian: 5 killed, 9 wounded. Enemy: 18 killed.

12 Platoon, D Company, 4 RAR/NZ, leaving its night position, was fired on from a well-established bunker system of the 33rd North Vietnamese Regiment's 3rd Battalion; one Australian was killed at once. Through the morning artillery, gunships and aircraft attacked the system, bringing fire within 40 metres. In the afternoon the company assaulted, and the leading section was cut down by fire from the flanks 15 metres short of the bunkers; the company withdrew, leaving its dead, and while pulling back ran into a second bunker system, where it was pinned down until dark with artillery brought within 100 metres. Five Australians were killed and nine wounded; eighteen enemy were killed, half of them by air attack. With V Company, the company recovered its dead and equipment two days later.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=4652) · Report's sources: Intsum, AWM95 1/4/235.pdf p154; AWM 290, 3/569/71/11 pt 2 Ivanhoe; AWM279, 569/R4/143, Combat Operations After Action Report, Operation Ivanhoe; AWM95 7-4-53.pdf pg30 Contact Rept;

### 21 September 1971: Bunker, Operation Ivanhoe

*Significant* · 4 RAR in contact

Australian: 15 wounded. Enemy: 7 killed, 6 wounded.

6 Platoon, B Company, 4 RAR/NZ, following a telephone wire, fired on an enemy soldier rolling it up and followed his tracks. A party looking for a night position then met enemy on three sides, and the main body was hit by rifle and 60 mm mortar fire, taking heavy casualties; 4 Platoon came up to join it, killing one enemy on the way. Artillery, helicopter teams and air strikes of napalm, bombs and rockets supported the company. Fifteen Australians were wounded, three staying on duty; the enemy were thought to be the headquarters and support weapons of the 33rd North Vietnamese Regiment, and two bunker complexes were found around the contact area.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=4655) · Report's sources: Intsum, AWM95 1/4/235.pdf p154; AWM 279 569/R4/143; AWM95 7-4-53.pdf pg39 Contact Rept;

### 2 November 1971: Patrol, Operation U/K

*Last* · 4 RAR in contact

5 Platoon, B Company, 4 RAR/NZ, was fired on by two or three enemy in green and black, who withdrew. There were no Australian casualties.
