## Notable contacts

### 23 October 1965: Deployment, Operation 13/65

*First* · Fire support for 1 RAR

Operation 13/65, 1 RAR's search-and-destroy operation north-east of the US 173rd Airborne Brigade's base at Bien Hoa, with a troop of armoured personnel carriers, 3 Field Troop, and 105 Field Battery and New Zealand's 161 Battery in direct support.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=10185) · Report's sources: AWM95, item 7/1/35, 1 Battalion Royal Australian Regiment, Narrative Annexes, 1-31 Oct 65, p. 18-19. McNeill, To Long Tan, p. 441.

### 8 November 1965: Arty/Mor, Operation 14/65

*Typical: artillery and mortar fire* · 1 Fd Regt in contact

Enemy: 2 killed.

105 Field Battery fired a battery mission from Landing Zone Queen in support of the US 1/503rd Battalion, which had met a Viet Cong main-force unit north-west of the Dong Nai River. The airborne controller confirmed two Viet Cong killed.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=10188) · Report's sources: AWM95, item 7/1/39, 1 Battalion Royal Australian Regiment, Narrative, Annexes, 1-30 Nov 1965, p. 11.

### 16 March 1966: Attack by en, Operation Silver City

*Typical: enemy attack* · 1 Fd Regt in contact

The US 2/503rd Battalion was attacked from all sides of its perimeter for four hours, after a bombardment of mortars, a howitzer and recoilless rifles, probably by three battalions of the 271st Regiment. Artillery, including 105 Field Battery, and air strikes played a large part in defeating the attack. The Americans lost 7 killed and 114 wounded; 288 Viet Cong were counted dead. A large cache of weapons was found the next day.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=10189) · Report's sources: AWM95, item 7/1/61, 1 Battalion, Royal Australian Regiment, Narrative, Annexes, 1-31 Mar 66, p. 20.

### 17 August 1966: Security, Operation TAOR

*Significant* · Fire support for 161 Field Battery, RNZA (New Zealand)

Australian: 1 killed, 24 wounded.

The Viet Cong shelled the Task Force's artillery lines at Nui Dat with about 66 rounds from 82 mm mortars and what was thought to be a 70 mm howitzer, killing one Australian and wounding 24. The artillery returned fire within seven minutes. A patrol from B Company, 6 RAR, later found the firing positions, with signs of a hurried departure, blood trails and carts used to take bodies away. This was the night before the Battle of Long Tan.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=218) · Report's sources: Intsum, AWM95 1/4/6.pdf p112-14;

### 18 August 1966: Patrol, Operation Smithfield (original - Vendetta)

*Significant* · Fire support for 6 RAR

Australian: 17 killed, 19 wounded. Enemy: 245 killed, 350 wounded.

The Battle of Long Tan. D Company, 6 RAR, 108 strong, was following fresh tracks east of Nui Dat when 11 Platoon met a few Viet Cong and then a much larger force. From about 5.30 pm the company was attacked by at least a company, later found to be far more, under heavy machine-gun and mortar fire in heavy rain, and held on until A Company arrived in armoured personnel carriers after 7 pm. Close artillery fire broke up many of the attacks; low cloud ruled out air strikes, and helicopters flew in ammunition and took out the wounded. Seventeen Australians were killed and nineteen wounded; 245 enemy were recorded killed. The company commander afterwards reported that the M60 machine-guns jammed in the mud and that the men needed to carry more ammunition.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=220) · Report's sources: To Long Tan; Sitrep, AWM95 1/4/6.pdf p56-8; AWM 102, 42 6RAR After Action Report, op. Smithfield, 18 -21 August 1966, Nui Dat (Battle of Long Tan)

### 18 August 1966: Contact, Operation Smithfield (original - Vendetta)

*Significant* · Fire support for 6 RAR

Australian: 4 wounded. Enemy: 20 killed, 40 wounded.

During the Battle of Long Tan, A Company, 6 RAR, rode out from Nui Dat in the armoured personnel carriers of 3 Troop, 1 APC Squadron, to relieve D Company. On the way it clashed with the support company of the D445 Battalion, causing about 15 casualties, and then with two companies of the 275th Regiment, causing about 45 more, before joining D Company as the enemy withdrew. Four Australians were wounded.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=222) · Report's sources: To Long Tan; Sitrep, AWM95 1/4/6.pdf p56-8; AWM 102, 42 6RAR After Action Report, op. Smithfield, 18 -21 August 1966; AWM95 7-6-5.pdf 6 RAR Aug 66;

### 10 February 1967: Security, Operation TAOR

*Typical: security* · 1 Fd Regt in contact

A standing patrol near 103 Field Battery heard noises to its south for 20 minutes. Believing it had been given away, the patrol leader moved it 150 metres north to the battery's outer wire; about half an hour later the enemy threw a grenade into the patrol.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=4717) · Report's sources: AWM109 R723/1/1/2 Pt1 HQ 1 ATF Reports General, Contact Rept; Intsum, AWM95 1/4/30.pdf p129;

### 17 February 1967: Bunker, Operation Bribie

*Significant* · Fire support for 6 RAR

Australian: 8 killed, 21 wounded. Enemy: 50 killed, 13 wounded.

Operation Bribie. After A Company, 6 RAR, found a Viet Cong company and its heavy weapons company dug into bunkers, B Company was ordered to assault the position. It was pinned down by fire from all sides, including heavy machine-guns, Claymore mines and mortars; four air strikes were called in, and carriers of 2 Troop, A Squadron, 3 Cavalry Regiment, went in to bring out the wounded, one being disabled by recoilless-rifle fire. After four hours both companies withdrew into the battalion's night position, and the enemy left during the night. Eight Australians were killed and 21 wounded; about 50 enemy were recorded killed. The battalion's commander said that tanks could have taken the position with little difficulty.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=447) · Report's sources: AWM 279, 723/R5/34; Scorpion Stings pg48-54; On the Offensive pg114. AWM95 7-6-11 pdf 6 RAR Op Bribie; AWM95 7-6-14.pdf pg189 Contact Rept;

### 6 June 1969: Contact, Operation Hammer

*Significant* · Fire support for 5 RAR

Australian: 1 killed, 10 wounded. Enemy: 43 killed, 6 wounded.

The Battle of Binh Ba. B and D Companies of 5 RAR, with tanks of B Squadron, 1 Armoured Regiment, carriers of B Squadron, 3 Cavalry Regiment, and local Regional and Popular Force troops, fought an estimated 90 enemy in the village of Binh Ba from late morning until evening. One Australian was killed and eight to ten wounded, and two Centurion tanks were damaged; 27 enemy were counted dead, more were recorded later, and two were captured.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=2496) · Report's sources: Sitrep, AWM95 1/4/156.pdf p16; Analysis of Aust Urban Engagements in Vn; Scorpion Stings pg169-78;

### 7 June 1969: Contact, Operation Hammer/Tong

*Significant* · Fire support for 5 RAR

Enemy: 40 killed.

A company of the 33rd North Vietnamese Regiment attacked Duc Trung and overwhelmed its local defence platoon. B Company, 5 RAR, was sent to retake it: artillery fire was brought within 20 metres of the houses, helicopter gunships attacked the north and west of the village, and B Company attacked from the south with a troop of tanks. The enemy were routed, and local forces cleared the rest of the village to avoid civilian casualties. Forty enemy were recorded killed.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=2509) · Report's sources: AWM 279 723/R5/59; AWM95 7-5-25.pdf pg36; Analysis of Australian Urban Engagements in Vietnam, R Hall and A Ross, ADSC, Unsw@ADFA May 2003;

### 11 June 1969: Bunker, Operation Lavarack

*Significant* · Fire support for 6 RAR

Australian: 10 wounded. Enemy: 9 killed.

B Company, 6 RAR, spotted a sentry and sent a platoon and a half to attack what seemed a small camp, their approach hidden by heavy rain. They surprised the enemy and killed the sentry, but the camp proved to be a large bunker system, and the enemy counter-attacked. The whole company was committed, with artillery, helicopter gunships and a gunship aircraft overhead for over an hour; after ten were wounded, the company was ordered to withdraw, hampered by rain and eight to ten snipers in the trees. Two machine-guns failed and radio contact was poor. Nine enemy bodies were found when the company went back in the next day to an empty camp.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=2541) · Report's sources: Intsum, AWM95 1/4/156.pdf p110; Sitrep AWM 98 (323); AWM95 7-6-22.pdf pg29 Contact Rept; AWM95 7-6-18.pdf pg102

### 21 August 1969: Bunker, Operation Camden 2

*Significant* · Fire support for 5 RAR

Australian: 1 killed, 37 wounded. Enemy: 9 killed, 25 wounded.

A Company, 5 RAR's Assault Pioneer Platoon heard voices ahead and, with 3 Platoon, assaulted a bunker system after artillery fire behind it to cut off the enemy. They met very heavy rocket and rifle fire from the bunkers and flanks, and pulled back to a clearing to evacuate the wounded; as the medical helicopters arrived, the enemy closed in and fired mortars, rifles and rockets into the wounded, until helicopter gunships drove them back and the evacuation was moved. One Australian was killed and more than 30 wounded; nine enemy were recorded killed and 25 wounded. The company commander said the radio net had been chaotic with every supporting element on it.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=2833) · Report's sources: AWM 288, 569/1/112/2 Pt 2 Camden; Intsum, AWM95 1/4/164.pdf p164; Sitrep, AWM95 1/4/164.pdf p74-5; AWM95 7-5-32.pdf pg176 Contact Rept;
