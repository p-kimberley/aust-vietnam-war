## Notable contacts

### 6 March 1968: Patrol

*First* · 1 Armd Regt in contact

Australian: 1 wounded.

A tank of C Squadron, 1 Armoured Regiment, was hit by a rocket-propelled grenade, damaging the tank and wounding one Australian. Artillery fired on the area with unknown results.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=1042) · Report's sources: Phuoc Tuy Incident Database;

### 26 May 1968: Security, Operation Toan Thang 1

*Significant* · Tank support for 3 RAR

Australian: 3 killed, 16 wounded. Enemy: 6 killed, 30 wounded.

The 165th North Vietnamese Regiment attacked Fire Support Base Balmoral, held by 3 RAR with tanks and carriers: heavy mortar fire, followed by a ground attack by about a company, supported by rockets and machine-guns, from an estimated battalion group. Three Australians were killed and sixteen wounded; six enemy were recorded killed.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=1323) · Report's sources: McAulay pg237-8; Scorpion Stings pg 114-116; On the Offensive pg 384; AWM95 2-3-9.pdf pg36 Contact Rept AFV;

### 28 May 1968: Security, Operation Toan Thang 1

*Significant* · Tank support for 3 RAR

Australian: 1 killed, 8 wounded. Enemy: 55 killed, 10 wounded.

The North Vietnamese renewed their attack on Fire Support Base Balmoral with a heavy mortar and rocket bombardment and a ground attack by two battalions of the 165th Regiment. The attack broke up on the wire under artillery and helicopter gunship fire. One Australian was killed and six to eight wounded; about 47 to 55 enemy were killed and six captured.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=1330) · Report's sources: AWM95 2-3-9.pdf pg46 Contact Rept AFV; Scorpion Stings pg118 ; Intsum, AWM95 1/4/100.pdf p122;

### 30 May 1968: Bunker, Operation Toan Thang 1

*Significant* · Tank support for 1 RAR

Australian: 1 killed, 7 wounded. Enemy: 24 killed, 8 wounded.

On a reconnaissance in force near Coral, C Company, 1 RAR, was pinned down by heavy mortar, machine-gun and rocket fire from a bunker system, at ranges of 10 to 15 metres, too close for gunships and artillery to help much, and carriers could not get through the jungle. Two tanks arrived from Coral and an assault destroyed eight bunkers; a carrier was hit by a rocket while taking out the wounded. The company then broke contact for air strikes. One Australian was killed and seven wounded; 24 enemy were killed and more probably killed, and five bodies were found when the company returned days later.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=1333) · Report's sources: Scorpion Stings pg119; McAulay pg289; On the Offensive pg394; AWM95 7-1-78 pt 1 .pdf 1 RAR May 68 pg25; AWM95 7-1-79.pdf 1 RAR Jun 68 pg71; AWM95 2-3-9.pdf pg53 Contact Rept AFV;

### 22 August 1968: Attack, Operation Nowra

*Significant* · Tank support for 1 RAR

Australian: 12 wounded. Enemy: 40 killed, 8 wounded.

About 100 enemy, of the D445 Battalion, attacked Long Dien with recoilless rifles, rocket-propelled grenades and automatic weapons. B and C Companies of 1 RAR, with tanks of C Squadron, 1 Armoured Regiment, carriers and helicopter gunships, fought a company-sized force in the town and another group on Route 23, in contacts that went on all day. About eleven Australians were wounded, most of C Company's by a 60 mm mortar; 29 to 40 enemy were recorded killed.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=1503) · Report's sources: Intsum, AWM1/4/113 pt3.pdf p44; See Urban Warfare Rept. AWM95 7-1-86.pdf 1 RAR Aug 68 pg15-16; AWM95 2-3-12.pdf pg43 Contact Rept AFV;

### 13 May 1969: Contact, Operation Reynella

*Typical: mine incident* · 1 Armd Regt in contact

Australian: 1 killed, 1 wounded.

A tank of B Squadron, 1 Armoured Regiment, set off a mine on a track south-east of Long Dien, an 80-pound Chinese TNT charge. One Australian was killed and one wounded, and the tank could not move under its own power.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=2385) · Report's sources: Intsum, AWM95 1/4/152.pdf p120; AWM95 7-9-16.pdf 9 RAR May 69 pg83, 85; AWM95 2-2-4.pdf pg10; AWM121, item 16, ARMD Incidents SVN no86;

### 27 May 1969: Ambush, Operation Mailed Fist

*Typical: ambush* · 1 Armd Regt in contact

Enemy: 1 killed, 6 wounded.

Tanks of B Squadron, 1 Armoured Regiment, and D and E Platoon engaged about twenty enemy. One enemy was killed and six wounded, and two rifles, 2,000 rounds of ammunition and two anti-tank grenades were captured.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=2443) · Report's sources: Intsum, AWM95 1/4/152.pdf p148; AWM95 2-2-4.pdf pg17;

### 6 June 1969: Contact, Operation Hammer

*Significant* · Tank support for 5 RAR

Australian: 1 killed, 10 wounded. Enemy: 43 killed, 6 wounded.

The Battle of Binh Ba. B and D Companies of 5 RAR, with tanks of B Squadron, 1 Armoured Regiment, carriers of B Squadron, 3 Cavalry Regiment, and local Regional and Popular Force troops, fought an estimated 90 enemy in the village of Binh Ba from late morning until evening. One Australian was killed and eight to ten wounded, and two Centurion tanks were damaged; 27 enemy were counted dead, more were recorded later, and two were captured.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=2496) · Report's sources: Sitrep, AWM95 1/4/156.pdf p16; Analysis of Aust Urban Engagements in Vn; Scorpion Stings pg169-78;

### 7 June 1969: Contact, Operation Hammer/Tong

*Significant* · Tank support for 5 RAR

Enemy: 40 killed.

A company of the 33rd North Vietnamese Regiment attacked Duc Trung and overwhelmed its local defence platoon. B Company, 5 RAR, was sent to retake it: artillery fire was brought within 20 metres of the houses, helicopter gunships attacked the north and west of the village, and B Company attacked from the south with a troop of tanks. The enemy were routed, and local forces cleared the rest of the village to avoid civilian casualties. Forty enemy were recorded killed.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=2509) · Report's sources: AWM 279 723/R5/59; AWM95 7-5-25.pdf pg36; Analysis of Australian Urban Engagements in Vietnam, R Hall and A Ross, ADSC, Unsw@ADFA May 2003;

### 6 August 1969: Security

*Typical: security* · 1 Armd Regt in contact

Australian: 4 killed.

Tanks of B Squadron, 1 Armoured Regiment, at Duc Thanh saw five enemy 100 metres away. A sweep with searchlights found lights, which were engaged with canister rounds and artillery.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=2752) · Report's sources: Intsum, AWM95 1/4/164.pdf p124; AWM121, item 16, ARMD Incidents SVN no86;

### 15 February 1970: Ambush, Operation Hammersley

*Significant* · Tank support for 8 RAR

Australian: 3 wounded. Enemy: 10 killed, 22 wounded.

9 Platoon, C Company, 8 RAR, was carried by tanks to within a kilometre of a carefully chosen ambush site and slipped in unseen while the tanks made a diversion, with engineers checking the site for mines and 15 Claymores laid along the track. It let the first of four enemy groups pass, and wiped out the second and third with its Claymores; the first and fourth counter-attacked. Running low on ammunition, the platoon was supported by mortars, artillery and helicopter gunships, and within 40 minutes the tanks of 1 Armoured Regiment came back, cleared the killing ground and stayed through the night. Three Australians were wounded; ten enemy were counted dead, and 22 drag marks were found.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=3516) · Report's sources: Scorpion Stings pg205; Intsum, AWM95 1/4/181.pdf Pt2 p172; AWM95 7-8-7.pdf 8 RAR Feb 70 pg87 Contact Rept; Sitrep, AWM95 1/4/181.pdf Pt2 p51

### 18 February 1970: Bunker, Operation Hammersley

*Significant* · Tank support for 8 RAR

Australian: 2 killed, 13 wounded. Enemy: 12 killed, 10 wounded.

C Company, 8 RAR, captured a Viet Cong who said 40 enemy were in a nearby bunker system protected by mines. The company attacked with carriers of B Squadron, 3 Cavalry Regiment, and tanks of 1 Armoured Regiment; a carrier was destroyed by two rocket-propelled grenades, killing two Australians and wounding more. After air and artillery strikes a second assault led by the tanks went through the bunkers to a deep creek, but the system was also protected by an anti-tank minefield, and the force was ordered to withdraw, taking more casualties from enemy who had reoccupied the bunkers. Twelve enemy were killed, mostly by the tanks' canister and high-explosive rounds; one tank was hit eight times and kept fighting. The system was later hit by B-52 bombers.

### 21 February 1970: Patrol, Operation Hammersley

*Typical: patrol* · 1 Armd Regt in contact

Australian: 2 wounded.

A tank of A Squadron, 1 Armoured Regiment, struck a 20-pound shaped-charge anti-tank mine in the jungle near the Long Hai Hills. The tank was put out of action and two of its crew were wounded.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=3550) · Report's sources: Sitrep, AWM95 1/4/181 Pt2.pdf p66; AWM95 7-8-7.pdf 8 RAR Feb 70 pg47; AWM95 2-1-4.pdf pg84 (Sitrep)

### 5 August 1971: Contact

*Last* · 1 Armd Regt in contact

A tank of 1 Troop, C Squadron, 1 Armoured Regiment, set off a 40-pound mine on a jungle track east of Cam My. The mine had probably been in place a long time, with a recently fitted pressure trigger. There were no casualties, but the tank's suspension and four road wheels were damaged.
