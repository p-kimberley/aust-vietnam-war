### 25 July 1966: Ambush, Operation Hobart

*Typical: ambush* · 6 RAR in contact

Australian: 3 wounded. Enemy: 2 killed, 9 wounded.

C Company, 6 RAR, was part of a two-company cordon, with two other companies driving the enemy towards it, when it met an estimated Viet Cong company. As the enemy withdrew, covered by a rearguard of about 15, C Company called in artillery; two Australians were wounded, which the battalion's commander put down to a platoon mistaking its own position rather than to the artillery. The company then came under mortar fire and fought several small-arms clashes, hitting about four enemy, and found a large bunker system nearby. Four enemy bodies killed by artillery were recovered over the following days.

### 25 July 1966: Ambush, Operation Hobart

*Significant* · 6 RAR in contact

Australian: 2 killed, 12 wounded. Enemy: 6 killed, 15 wounded.

B Company, 6 RAR, was manning a cut-off line on a track in a battalion cordon when its sentries opened fire on two groups of Viet Cong, probably most of a company. 6 Platoon brought artillery down on the enemy, but when it rose to clear the area it was hit by rifle and mortar fire, and one Australian shell fell short into the company's position, killing two and wounding several. By the time the enemy withdrew the company had two killed and twelve wounded; at least six enemy were killed and about fifteen wounded. The commanders afterwards debated whether the troops' strict fire discipline had let the enemy's fire go unanswered for too long.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=181) · Report's sources: AWM279 569/R4/28; AWM95 7-6-4.pdf 6 RAR Jul 66; AWM95 1-4-11.pdf pg21-7, HQ 1 ATF, Contact Rept;

### 18 August 1966: Patrol, Operation Smithfield (original - Vendetta)

*Significant* · 6 RAR in contact

Australian: 17 killed, 19 wounded. Enemy: 245 killed, 350 wounded.

The Battle of Long Tan. D Company, 6 RAR, 108 strong, was following fresh tracks east of Nui Dat when 11 Platoon met a few Viet Cong and then a much larger force. From about 5.30 pm the company was attacked by at least a company, later found to be far more, under heavy machine-gun and mortar fire in heavy rain, and held on until A Company arrived in armoured personnel carriers after 7 pm. Close artillery fire broke up many of the attacks; low cloud ruled out air strikes, and helicopters flew in ammunition and took out the wounded. Seventeen Australians were killed and nineteen wounded; 245 enemy were recorded killed. The company commander afterwards reported that the M60 machine-guns jammed in the mud and that the men needed to carry more ammunition.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=220) · Report's sources: To Long Tan; Sitrep, AWM95 1/4/6.pdf p56-8; AWM 102, 42 6RAR After Action Report, op. Smithfield, 18 -21 August 1966, Nui Dat (Battle of Long Tan)

### 18 August 1966: Contact, Operation Smithfield (original - Vendetta)

*Significant* · 6 RAR in contact

Australian: 4 wounded. Enemy: 20 killed, 40 wounded.

During the Battle of Long Tan, A Company, 6 RAR, rode out from Nui Dat in the armoured personnel carriers of 3 Troop, 1 APC Squadron, to relieve D Company. On the way it clashed with the support company of the D445 Battalion, causing about 15 casualties, and then with two companies of the 275th Regiment, causing about 45 more, before joining D Company as the enemy withdrew. Four Australians were wounded.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=222) · Report's sources: To Long Tan; Sitrep, AWM95 1/4/6.pdf p56-8; AWM 102, 42 6RAR After Action Report, op. Smithfield, 18 -21 August 1966; AWM95 7-6-5.pdf 6 RAR Aug 66;

### 2 December 1966: Patrol, Operation Ingham

*Typical: patrol* · 6 RAR in contact

Australian: 1 killed, 10 wounded.

A command-detonated mine exploded against D Company, 6 RAR, killing one Australian and wounding ten, with three civilians also wounded. About half the casualties, including the man killed, were caused by an Australian Claymore mine carried by an NCO, which went off in the blast. The record also notes evidence that a Vietnamese woman who ran to the scene was wounded or killed by soldiers who mistook her for an enemy.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=368) · Report's sources: AWM279 569/R4/40;Intsum, AWM95 2/6/13.pdf p127; AWM95 7-6-8.pdf 6 RAR Nov 66;

### 17 February 1967: Bunker, Operation Bribie

*Significant* · 6 RAR in contact

Australian: 6 wounded. Enemy: 6 killed, 4 wounded.

6 RAR was being flown in by helicopter to cut off enemy forces retreating from an attack on Phuoc Hai. 2 Platoon, A Company, pushing into the jungle to secure the landing zone, came under sniper fire and then ran into a Viet Cong company dug into bunkers with overhead cover. Six Australians were wounded, five of them by an automatic weapon; three to six enemy were killed. The platoon withdrew 150 metres.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=445) · Report's sources: AWM 279, 723/R5/34, Bunker Attack Data base; Intel V-dat Base; AWM95 7-6-11 pdf 6 RAR Op Bribie; AWM95 7-6-14.pdf pg187 Contact Rept; Intsum, AWM95 1/4/30.pdf p138;

### 17 February 1967: Bunker, Operation Bribie

*Significant* · 6 RAR in contact

Australian: 8 killed, 21 wounded. Enemy: 50 killed, 13 wounded.

Operation Bribie. After A Company, 6 RAR, found a Viet Cong company and its heavy weapons company dug into bunkers, B Company was ordered to assault the position. It was pinned down by fire from all sides, including heavy machine-guns, Claymore mines and mortars; four air strikes were called in, and carriers of 2 Troop, A Squadron, 3 Cavalry Regiment, went in to bring out the wounded, one being disabled by recoilless-rifle fire. After four hours both companies withdrew into the battalion's night position, and the enemy left during the night. Eight Australians were killed and 21 wounded; about 50 enemy were recorded killed. The battalion's commander said that tanks could have taken the position with little difficulty.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=447) · Report's sources: AWM 279, 723/R5/34; Scorpion Stings pg48-54; On the Offensive pg114. AWM95 7-6-11 pdf 6 RAR Op Bribie; AWM95 7-6-14.pdf pg189 Contact Rept;

### 11 June 1969: Bunker, Operation Lavarack

*Significant* · 6 RAR in contact

Australian: 10 wounded. Enemy: 9 killed.

B Company, 6 RAR, spotted a sentry and sent a platoon and a half to attack what seemed a small camp, their approach hidden by heavy rain. They surprised the enemy and killed the sentry, but the camp proved to be a large bunker system, and the enemy counter-attacked. The whole company was committed, with artillery, helicopter gunships and a gunship aircraft overhead for over an hour; after ten were wounded, the company was ordered to withdraw, hampered by rain and eight to ten snipers in the trees. Two machine-guns failed and radio contact was poor. Nine enemy bodies were found when the company went back in the next day to an empty camp.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=2541) · Report's sources: Intsum, AWM95 1/4/156.pdf p110; Sitrep AWM 98 (323); AWM95 7-6-22.pdf pg29 Contact Rept; AWM95 7-6-18.pdf pg102

### 20 June 1969: Ambush, Operation Lavarack

*Significant* · 6 RAR in contact

Enemy: 22 killed.

10 Platoon, D Company, 6 RAR, had been in ambush in thick bamboo beside a track through the Courtenay rubber plantation for two days when about 60 enemy, a carrying party with stretcher-bearers and escort, walked into it. The Claymores were fired with as many as possible in the killing ground, followed by three machine-guns; the enemy fired a few wild shots and fled. The sweep found 22 dead and captured weapons, grenades, medical supplies and documents. Five of the eleven Claymores failed from faulty detonators, but the machine-guns, cleaned daily, fired 500 rounds each without a stoppage.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=2566) · Report's sources: Intsum, AWM95 1/4/156.pdf p129; Sitrep AWM 98 (323); AWM95 7-6-22.pdf pg103 Contact Rept;

### 21 July 1969: Patrol, Operation Mundingburra

*Significant* · 6 RAR in contact

Australian: 1 killed, 18 wounded.

While 3 Platoon, A Company, 6 RAR, was clearing a harbour position half a kilometre south-east of Hoi My, the platoon commander stepped out of the cleared area and set off an M16 mine. He was killed and 18 were wounded, one of them a sapper. 750 pounds of rice was found nearby.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=2697) · Report's sources: Intsum, AWM95 1/4/160.pdf p142; AWM95 7-6-24.pdf pg95

### 21 July 1969: Security, Operation Mundingburra

*Typical: security* · 6 RAR in contact

Australian: 1 killed, 5 wounded.

While the scene of the earlier mine blast near Hoi My was being cleared, another M16 mine went off, killing one Australian and wounding five, among them the battalion's medical officer and its commanding officer.

[Open on the Battle Map](/battlemap?incident=2698) · Report's sources: AWM95 7-6-24.pdf pg95

### 28 February 1970: Bunker, Operation Gisborne

*Typical: bunker* · 6 RAR in contact

Australian: 9 wounded. Enemy: 4 wounded.

Elements of 6 RAR/NZ attacked a new base of about 50 bunkers that the D445 Battalion had been building for a week since 8 RAR drove it from the Minh Dam base in the Long Hai Hills. 3 Platoon, looking for the camp, saw a well-used track and a poncho, and both forward sections came under intense rocket, machine-gun and rifle fire, including heavy machine-guns. They pulled back 100 metres while artillery, mortars and helicopter gunships hit the area; nine Australians were wounded, and the enemy withdrew, leaving one dead and many blood trails. The commanders noted that dense undergrowth limited their rockets and grenade launchers, and that navigation errors made support fire less effective.
